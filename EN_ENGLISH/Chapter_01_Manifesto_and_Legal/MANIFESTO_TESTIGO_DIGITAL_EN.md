# CALL TO THE COLOMBIAN AND INTERNATIONAL CIVIL SOCIETY

**FOR THE AUTHENTICITY, INTEGRITY, TRACEABILITY, AND INDEPENDENT VERIFIABILITY OF THE ELECTRONIC DOCUMENTS SUPPORTING THE 2026 PRESIDENTIAL ELECTION**

**Bogotá, D. C., August 2, 2026**

The citizens, electoral witnesses, professionals, academics, and civil society organizations who subscribe to or adhere to this declaration, exercising our freedoms of expression and information, our right to participate in the formation, exercise, and control of political power, and our civic duties to defend democratic institutions, wish to communicate the following considerations to Colombia and the international community.

This declaration does not formulate criminal charges against any person, does not attribute individual or collective responsibilities, and does not intend to anticipate the decisions corresponding to judicial authorities.

Its purpose is strictly constitutional, civic, technical, and democratic: to highlight the necessity that the electronic documents supporting a presidential election must be able to demonstrate, through objective, open, and independent mechanisms, their authenticity, integrity, traceability, and correspondence with the original documents produced by the voting juries.

---

### FIRST: A CITIZEN CALL IN DEFENSE OF ELECTORAL VERIFIABILITY
More than 75,000 citizens, inside and outside Colombia, participated in the surveillance, preservation, download, and examination of the electoral information published by the competent authorities during the electoral process held on June 21, 2026.
This citizen effort aimed to exercise the constitutional right and duty to observe, examine, and control the functioning of public power, especially when the election of the President of the Republic is at stake.
The citizenry is not requesting technological privileges or impossible conditions, but rather the preservation and verifiability of constitutional guarantees that are fundamental in any modern electronic documentation system.

### SECOND: THE ELECTRONIC DOCUMENT MUST BE ABLE TO DEMONSTRATE ITS AUTHENTICITY AND INTEGRITY
Article 95 of Law 270 of 1996, Statutory Law of the Administration of Justice, establishes that documents issued by electronic means shall enjoy the validity and efficacy of an original document provided that their authenticity, integrity, and compliance with the requirements demanded by procedural laws are guaranteed.
This requirement predates even Law 527 of 1999 and demonstrates that the authenticity and integrity of an electronic document, beyond formal IT requirements, constitute essential legal conditions for such a document to be valued, used, and accepted as reliable support for a public or judicial action.

In the digital environment, a document is not reliable solely because an authority publishes it, because it can be opened on a computer, or because it is viewed as an image or a PDF file. Its legal and evidentiary reliability depends on it preserving objective technical elements that allow establishing:
— who or what system produced the document;
— when it was generated, received, signed, digitized, or published;
— whether its content remained intact and unaltered since its creation;
— whether there is an unequivocal correspondence between all the pages and components that comprise it;
— whether its origin, processing, transmission, storage, and documentary history can be reconstructed;
— and whether these conditions can be verified by an independent third party.

These demands are not a technical extravagance or a standard invented exclusively for the electoral process, but the very same guarantees upon which the ordinary functioning of the State, the national economy, and contemporary international commerce rest.

### THIRD: CITIZEN CONCERN OVER THE E-14 FORMS PUBLISHED IN 2026
The citizen examination of the digitized and published E-14 forms during the 2026 presidential election has allowed the identification of circumstances that require a rigorous judicial, technical, and institutional assessment.
Among these are the absence or insufficiency of verifiable metadata; the lack of publicly verifiable cryptographic timestamps; the impossibility of validating certain alphanumeric strings as integrity mechanisms; the loss of documentary identifiers, and the separation between the pages containing the results and those displaying the signatures of the voting juries.

These elements allow the formulation of a distinct, legally relevant, and democratically unavoidable question:
**Do the electronic E-14 forms published in 2026 retain the technical mechanisms necessary to demonstrate, to any citizen and through independent verification, that they correspond exactly to the documents produced by the voting juries and that they remained unaltered since their digitization?**

This declaration asserts that the electoral system must preserve and exhibit the objective elements that allow demonstrating that they were not altered. This is not about accusing. This is about verifying.

### FOURTH: PUBLISHING INFORMATION IS NOT ENOUGH: IT MUST ALSO BE VERIFIABLE
Democratic transparency does not solely consist of allowing a citizen to download a file. It consists of allowing that citizen to verify, through reproducible technical procedures, that the downloaded file is authentic, complete, corresponds to the originally produced document, and was not substituted or modified during its processing, storage, or publication.

When only the electoral authority and its technological operators preserve the information necessary to perform this verification, while the citizenry lacks access to it, a profound asymmetry of verifiability is produced.
Under these conditions, public trust ceases to rest on verifiable evidence and becomes exclusively dependent on the word of those who administer the systems and control the information.
That cannot be the standard of a constitutional democracy.

### FIFTH: THE VERIFIABILITY OF EACH E-14 AFFECTS THE RELIABILITY OF THE ACCUMULATED RESULT
The loss of verifiability of individual documents compromises the ability to objectively demonstrate the correspondence between the original documents, the digitized files, the transmitted data, and the consolidated results.
In a presidential election, this demonstrative capacity cannot depend solely on closed systems, inaccessible databases, or subsequent explanations. It must rest on contemporary mechanisms of integrity, preservation, traceability, and independent auditing.

### SIXTH: THREE FINDINGS COMPROMISING INDEPENDENT VERIFIABILITY
**3.1. Separation between the results and the signatures of the juries**
In the published electronic versions—which are supposed to originate from the scanning of the original physical documents—some of the main visible identifiers that would allow relating each page to a specific polling station and election appear hidden, deleted, or substituted.
This loss of identifiers acquires special relevance when the separated pages also do not preserve verifiable metadata, a cryptographic timestamp, or a digital footprint (hash value) that uniquely links all the pages of the form.

**3.2. Forms marked as "CONFIDENTIAL" and tally sheets published without the signature page**
The presence of this watermark in electoral documents published for public consultation demands a precise institutional and technical explanation. The expression "CONFIDENTIAL" demonstrates an alteration of the PDF containing the E-14 form and constitutes an indication of a prior process of editing.
Forms were also found lacking the page containing the juries' signatures. The absence of the signature page prevents the citizen from verifying who signed the tally sheet.

**3.3. Absence of security controls and appearance of statistical sequences incompatible with a random distribution**
The statistical study (Benford's Law (2nd Digit - Mebane) and Variance) identified numerous polling locations exhibiting prolonged sequences of tables oriented in the same electoral direction, lacking the alternations that would normally arise when each table behaves independently.
In other words, the results did not appear distributed as an irregular succession of autonomous tables, but grouped into excessively extensive and repeated consecutive blocks. The reported statistical probability for a grouping of this magnitude to appear by mere chance is practically zero.

### SEVENTH: RESPECT FOR JUDICIAL INDEPENDENCE AND DUE PROCESS
The controversies related to the 2026 presidential election are submitted to the jurisdiction of the administrative courts (Jurisdicción de lo Contencioso Administrativo).
This declaration does not intend to pressure, substitute, or anticipate that decision. On the contrary, it expresses confidence that the technical and legal elements submitted for their consideration will be examined with depth, publicity, impartiality, and respect for due process.

### EIGHTH: REQUESTS TO ELECTORAL AUTHORITIES AND OTHER COMPETENT ENTITIES
We respectfully request the electoral authorities, control bodies, and other competent institutions to:
1. Preserve entirely the original physical forms, electronic files, source images, databases, transmission records, and metadata.
2. Guarantee the preservation of physical and digital chains of custody.
3. Explain the mechanisms used to guarantee the correspondence between the results and signature pages.
4. Facilitate the realization of independent, academic, and forensic audits.
5. Adopt for future electoral processes a public protocol for authenticity and traceability.

### NINTH: INVITATION TO UNIVERSITIES, EXPERTS, AND ORGANIZATIONS
Colombia has faculties, research centers, laboratories, organizations, and professionals of recognized prestige. We invite this academic and professional community to examine the problem with independence and scientific rigor.
The defense of digital democracy does not belong to a campaign, a party, or an ideological sector. It is a common responsibility.

### TENTH: COMMUNICATION TO NATIONAL AND INTERNATIONAL ORGANIZATIONS
This declaration is addressed, for their knowledge, to:
— The Inter-American Commission on Human Rights (IACHR);
— The United Nations Human Rights Committee;
— The Organization of American States (OAS);
— Electoral observation missions and organizations;
— The Congress of the Republic of Colombia;
— The control bodies of the Colombian State;
— The Colombian and international civil society.

### ELEVENTH: A DEMOCRACY CANNOT DEMAND BLIND FAITH IN ITS ELECTRONIC DOCUMENTS
It is not enough to state that a document is authentic. It must preserve the elements that allow demonstrating it.
A democracy cannot demand its citizens to blindly believe in the authenticity of the documents supporting the election of its President. It must be able to demonstrate it.

### TWELFTH: PUBLIC DISSEMINATION WAS THE LAST RESORT, NOT THE FIRST
The public dissemination of this evidence does not arise from a desire for confrontation. It is the consequence of a citizen process that began years ago, when thousands of people warned of risks to the verifiability of the electoral system and resorted, in the first place, to the institutional mechanisms provided.
It was precisely the exhaustion of these institutional avenues—and not ignorance of them—that led thousands of citizens to organize themselves to preserve, download, classify, analyze, and disseminate the available evidence.

### THIRTEENTH: THE PROBLEM IN THE TRADITION OF MODERN POLITICAL THOUGHT
Applied to the electoral sphere, this concern acquires a special gravity. Popular sovereignty is expressed through the vote, but its institutional efficacy depends on the documents used being objectively and independently verifiable.
As established by Rousseau and Locke, when the administrators of the State alter the rules and hide necessary information to control their actions, they breach the fiduciary mandate.

### FOURTEENTH: THE RESPONSE MUST OCCUR WITHIN THE CONSTITUTIONAL STATE
Citizens are not being asked to disregard institutions. Institutions are being asked to demonstrate, through verifiable evidence, that the exercise of power effectively corresponds to the will deposited in the ballot boxes.

### FINAL CALL TO THE CITIZENRY
We ask citizens inside and outside Colombia to contribute to the responsible dissemination of this communiqué and the public evidence that supports it.
Sharing this information constitutes a legitimate form of citizen participation and control. Its purpose is not to promote confrontations, prematurely delegitimize institutions, or attribute individual responsibilities.

Citizen dissemination does not substitute the role of judges nor does it intend to anticipate their decisions. We expressly request that this information not be used to single out, threaten, harass, or stigmatize public servants, voting juries, candidates, or political organizations.

Therefore, this is not a call against Colombian democracy. **It is a call in defense of it.**

To disseminate this communiqué is to help the evidence be known, examined, and discussed publicly. It is to ask that doubts be resolved through justice and not through silence. It is to defend the right of all Colombians—without political distinction—that the legitimacy of their institutions rests on authentic, whole, traceable, and verifiable information.
